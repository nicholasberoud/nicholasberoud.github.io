Where Is the Fed?

The short end is set in a committee room. The long end is not.

Around

This is the outline of a commentary I’m writing, not the commentary. It started after a weekend reading Bloomberg’s coverage of the Fed; the argument and every number in the finished piece will be my own, dated and sourced.

Commentary on the Fed usually asks one question: will it cut or not? For the bond market the more useful question is how much of today’s 10-year yield the Fed actually controls. This piece is about that gap.

01What it will answer

  1. What the Fed has done.The path of the policy rate this year, and what the September statement and projections said about where it goes next.
  2. What the market expects.The rate path priced in futures and the Treasury curve, set against the committee’s own projections. Where they disagree is the story.
  3. How much of the 10-year is policy.The split between expected short rates and the term premium, from the same New York Fed model used in The Return of the Bond Vigilantes.
  4. The balance sheet.Where the Fed’s holdings of Treasuries stand, and what that means for how much the private market has to absorb.
  5. The fiscal backdrop.Why the deficit matters for the long end even when the Fed sets the short end.

02House rules for commentary

Commentary on The Long End is shorter and quicker than an analysis, but it keeps three rules.

  • Every number has a date and a source. Market prices move by the hour; a level without a timestamp is not a fact.
  • What the market prices and what I think are kept apart. The first is evidence. The second is an opinion, labelled as one.
  • The strongest counter-view gets a paragraph. If I can’t state it fairly, I don’t understand the question yet.

03Sources it will use

  • Federal Reserve, FOMC meeting calendar, statements and projections.
  • Federal Reserve Bank of New York, ACM term premium estimates.
  • Federal Reserve, H.4.1 balance sheet release, and Treasury yields from FRED.

Repo Market Model

Evidence pack: 1 October 2026 · Page updated 1 November 2026

What can public data tell us about pressure in overnight Treasury funding markets?

Repo is the market where cash is borrowed against securities, including US Treasuries. Eleonora Björnberg’s project asks whether observable funding conditions can help forecast pressure in that market, using information available before the day being forecast.

My interest was in the economics behind the inputs. A movement in reserves, the Treasury’s cash balance or a money-market facility is a useful observation only if we understand what it measures and what else might explain it.

My contribution

I contributed data selection and economic interpretation and prepared an advisor evidence pack dated 1 October 2026. It brought together public data, historical comparisons and questions for the model’s design:

  • Interpreting funding pressure. I examined the Secured Overnight Financing Rate (SOFR) relative to interest on reserve balances (IORB), and how to interpret pressure thresholds across different market conditions.
  • Understanding the balance-sheet context. I examined reserves, overnight reverse repo (ON RRP) balances, Treasury cash movements and calendar effects around funding pressure.
  • Separating observations from explanations. The memo distinguishes computed observations from economic arguments, third-party commentary and claims requiring verification.

How to read the evidence

The pack uses historical FRED data downloaded on 1 October 2026. These are revised series, not a reconstruction of the information a forecaster could have known on each historical date. The analysis is descriptive: it does not establish causal effects or validate a forecasting signal.

Its public memo is an inspectable record of my contribution. The standalone code and data pipeline used to generate that memo are not public. The original model’s code, evaluation and current limitations are maintained separately in Eleonora’s repository.

Attribution and current work

Eleonora is the project’s author and leads its research design, model implementation, methodology and evaluation. My earlier advisory role is recorded in the contributor statement. I stepped back from ongoing responsibilities in October; the project’s current ownership record assigns data interpretation decisions to Eleonora.

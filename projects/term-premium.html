Term Premium Research Journal

Fiscal risk, Treasury demand and sovereign markets

Updated 20 October 2026

How much extra do investors ask for lending to the US government for ten years, and does that change when the government borrows more?

This journal started as my undergraduate thesis. My thesis has since moved to the Federal Reserve’s FIMA Repo Facility, and this work continues as an independent project: a series of dated, short articles, each built from public data and generated results, each saying plainly what it can and cannot show.

The term premium is the part of a long-term yield that is not explained by expected short-term rates. It cannot be observed directly; it is estimated by models, and different models give different answers. Every article says which estimate it uses.

Articles

  • How Stable Is the Link Between Deficits and the Term Premium?20 October 2026 · US data, 1980 to 2025

    From 1980 to 2007, a wider deficit went with a noticeably higher 10-year term premium. Since 2008 the link is much weaker. I check whether that holds up to corrected timing, fewer repeated observations and a second premium model.

How each article is structured

  1. The question and why it matters now
  2. The observation and the dated evidence
  3. My interpretation
  4. A competing explanation
  5. What the evidence cannot establish
  6. What would change my mind
  7. Sources and reproducibility

Questions I’m exploring next

These are open questions, not results. Each would become its own article once it has a design and data behind it.

  • What moved Treasury yields? A dated split of 10-year yield changes into expected rates and term premium, episode by episode.
  • From yields to the interest bill. How quickly higher rates pass through to federal interest costs as debt is refinanced.
  • Who holds the debt. Whether changes in Treasury ownership change how sensitive the premium is to fiscal conditions. The background is in Who Buys the Debt?
  • A fiscal-news diary. Market moves around one family of events, such as debt-limit deadlines or refunding announcements.
  • France as a separate case. The French-German ten-year spread measures something different from the US term premium, so it needs its own design rather than a copy of this one.

Data and code

The articles use public data from the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, the Federal Reserve Board and FRED. The analysis code is kept in a private research repository; each article publishes its generated results file, chart data and a record of the code version and data files used.

Treasury term premium & bond-market discipline

Page draft · 11 October 2026

Has the way Treasury markets respond to US fiscal deficits changed, and does it matter who holds the debt?

My International Relations thesis brings a political-economy question to fixed-income data: how central-bank institutions and the ownership of government debt may shape bond-market discipline.

I’m studying changes in the relationship between fiscal deficits and the ten-year Treasury term premium, the estimated compensation investors demand for bearing long-term interest-rate risk. The work considers the Federal Reserve’s balance sheet and changes in the Treasury buyer base as possible explanations.

Project page in preparation. This is an introduction to ongoing research, not a statement of established causality.

Questions I’m investigating

  • When did the relationship between deficits and term premium change?
  • Do changes in the buyer base help explain that shift?
  • How sensitive are the results to the measure of term premium, the fiscal series and the period studied?

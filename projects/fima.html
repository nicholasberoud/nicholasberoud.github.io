FIMA Repo Facility

Domestic protection through dollar liquidity abroad

Updated 20 October 2026

Why would the Federal Reserve lend dollars to foreign central banks against their own holdings of US Treasuries?

In March 2020, as dollar funding strained around the world, the Federal Reserve created a temporary facility that let foreign central banks and other monetary authorities with accounts at the New York Fed borrow dollars overnight against the Treasuries they held there, instead of selling those securities into a stressed market. In July 2021 the facility was made standing.

The direction I am exploring for my International Relations thesis is that a facility serving foreign official institutions can also be read as protection for the US market itself: dollar liquidity provided abroad, in part to keep Treasury market functioning orderly at home. What draws me to it is the institutional question of why a central bank with a domestic mandate builds a tool for foreign authorities, and on what terms.

Where this stands. The research question and methods are still developing with my supervisor. This page describes a direction, not findings.

FIMA Repo Facility

Domestic protection through dollar liquidity abroad

Updated 24 October 2026

Why would the Federal Reserve lend dollars to foreign central banks against their own holdings of US Treasuries?

In March 2020, as dollar funding strained around the world, the Federal Reserve created a temporary facility that let foreign central banks and other monetary authorities with accounts at the New York Fed borrow dollars overnight against the Treasuries they held there, instead of selling those securities into a stressed market. In July 2021 the facility was made standing.

The idea I explored as a possible International Relations thesis is that a facility serving foreign official institutions can also be read as protection for the US market itself: dollar liquidity provided abroad, in part to keep Treasury market functioning orderly at home. What draws me to it is the institutional question of why a central bank with a domestic mandate builds a tool for foreign authorities, and on what terms.

Where this stands. My thesis is now moving toward emerging-market sovereign credit: political credibility and sovereign credit. I keep this page as background, because FIMA is part of the international dollar safety net that question sits inside. It describes an idea, not findings.

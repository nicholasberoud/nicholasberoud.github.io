About me

I’m completing IE’s 3+1 program, combining a bachelor’s in International Relations at IE University with a Master in Finance at IE New York College. I’m part of the first MiF cohort in New York, where I also lead the Finance & Strategy Club.

I came to markets through political economy. International Relations taught me to ask how institutions and political choices change economic outcomes. Finance lets me follow that question one step further, to the price of a particular bond and the decision someone has to make about it. That is why I’m drawn to fixed income, and above all to emerging-market sovereign debt: can a government keep its commitments, what does the bond already price, and what would change the view?

My undergraduate thesis is developing around political credibility and sovereign borrowing in emerging markets. The question and empirical design remain open. My independent Treasury term-premium study is a separate project within The Long End. Both degrees are expected to finish in June 2027.

I also take part in hackathons, most recently finishing second with a team that priced disruption risk in a critical-mineral supply chain. In all of it I’m practising the same habit: say what the evidence shows, say what it can’t, and keep the two apart.

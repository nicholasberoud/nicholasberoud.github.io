Undergraduate thesis · IE University · Proposal stage

Legislative Constraints and Sovereign Risk: Domestic Institutions in Latin American Debt Markets

Proposal
Submitted to my advisor, November 2026
Stage
Awaiting feedback. Data work is on hold until then.
Findings
None. No data have been combined and no model has been estimated.
Expected
June 2027

Do Latin American governments whose legislatures can hold the executive to account borrow more cheaply in dollar bond markets, once their fiscal and economic conditions are taken into account?

The proposed question

The proposal asks whether stronger legislative constraints on the executive are associated with lower dollar-denominated sovereign spreads in Latin America, after accounting for fiscal and economic conditions. Estimates would be conditional associations. The design, sample and outcome measure are still to be agreed with my advisor.

A proposal, not a findings abstract.

Why it matters

A government can have the resources to repay and still not be believed. The thesis starts from that gap between ability and willingness to repay. A legislature that can question, investigate and constrain the executive may make a government’s commitments to creditors more credible. The same checks can also make it harder to change course when change is needed, so the direction of the relationship is an empirical question.

What the study would use

  • Sovereign risk: dollar-denominated sovereign bond spreads by country.
  • Institutions: V-Dem’s index of legislative constraints on the executive, v2xlg_legcon (codebook v16, pp. 53 to 54). It measures oversight in practice: questioning officials, investigations and opposition scrutiny.
  • Fiscal and economic conditions: public fiscal and macroeconomic indicators.

The main open data question is the spread measure. The proposal refers to bonds issued directly by national governments, and the most accessible country indices may also include quasi-sovereign issuers. Whether an available series is suitable, and over which countries and years, is part of the feedback I have asked for.

What it will not claim

  • Estimates will be conditional associations, not causal effects.
  • A spread is a market price, not a direct measure of a government’s willingness to repay.
  • An index of legislative constraints measures oversight in practice, not the intentions of the people exercising it.

How it got here

The project began in October with Ballard-Rosa, Mosley and Wellhausen’s Contingent Advantage?, which asks when democratic institutions help governments borrow. The current title and question come from the proposal I submitted in November. Earlier designs, including a comparison of how markets price fiscal repair against fiscal deterioration, are retired.

Next steps

  1. Take my advisor’s feedback on the question, the spread measure and the sample.
  2. Then build and check one panel of spreads, institutional and fiscal data.
  3. Pin down exactly how the study differs from the closest existing work.

Who Buys the Debt?

Updated 12 October 2026

A question about government borrowing quickly becomes a question about who is buying the debt.

My thesis began with the relationship between US fiscal deficits and Treasury term premium. Looking at that relationship raised another question: if the buyers change, should we expect the market’s response to stay the same?

The Institution Behind the Buyer

For the Federal Reserve, buying securities is a monetary-policy tool. Its purchases need to be understood in terms of policy objectives, not only the return on a particular bond. The Fed describes how its purchases during and after the financial crisis were used to put downward pressure on longer-term interest rates. Read the Fed’s explanation ↗

That distinction is what brings my International Relations background into this project. I’m interested in how an institution’s mandate and decision-making arrangements influence its actions, and how those actions affect markets beyond the institution itself.

The Question I’m Working Through

My working hypothesis is that changes in demand from policy-driven buyers may help explain changes in the relationship between fiscal deficits and term premium. Testing it means looking at ownership alongside other possible explanations, not treating the buyer base as an answer in advance.

A chart can show that two things moved together. Explaining why, and whether the explanation holds up in other periods, is the harder part.
